\section{Audit Methodology Details}
\label{app:audit}

\subsection{OOF independence taxonomy}
We distinguish three levels of evaluation independence:
(i) \emph{per-row fold isolation}---each evaluation row was absent
from the training fold of every channel that scored it;
(ii) \emph{upstream signal isolation}---no upstream artifact whose
construction consumed the evaluation row's label may feed the channel;
(iii) \emph{selection independence}---no hyperparameter, ensemble
weight, or checkpoint was chosen using the evaluation set.
Our headline score satisfied (i) only approximately---eight
gold-600 texts are exact duplicates that can cross folds---and failed
(ii) and (iii).

\paragraph{Leak A (transitive pseudo-label contamination).}
The deployed neutral-expert channel combines a frozen-feature MLP
head with a corpus-trained neutral detector
(\texttt{neu\_pseudo\_p60k\_e2}). That detector was trained on
115{,}000 pseudo-labelled corpus rows produced by \texttt{stu3c}, the
full-fit three-class student. \texttt{stu3c}'s training meta records
confirm all 600 gold rows were used
(\texttt{distill\_meta.json}, n=600). Because the pseudo-labels are a
deterministic function of \texttt{stu3c} outputs on corpus text, and
\texttt{stu3c} memorized gold labels, the detector's scores on gold
rows carry information about \emph{all} gold labels---not just its own
fold. We verified this numerically: the detector's stored gold
probabilities equal the \texttt{stu3c} gold probabilities elementwise
(max $|\Delta|=0.0$). In the deployed blend the corpus channel
contributes $0.4$ of the neutral-expert probability, which itself is
weighted $0.5$ in the final three-class mixture---an effective
$\approx$13\% of the output mass. Removing the channel
(\texttt{eval\_a100\_pipeline.py}, \texttt{w\_corpus=0}) changes the
three-class score by $\approx$0.7pp (84.0\% $\to$ 83.3\%), bounding
Leak A's contribution.

\paragraph{Leak B (second-order teacher leakage).}
Every distillation channel was trained with soft targets
$\alpha{=}0.5$ produced by teacher ensembles whose own predictions on
the student's validation fold were generated by models trained on
that same fold (the teacher fold assignment is identical by
construction). The student therefore received, through the soft
targets, information about its validation labels beyond what the
hard-label loss permits. We bound this by retraining all three tasks
(3-class, binary, neutral) with $\alpha{=}1.0$ (hard labels only) and
rebuilding the full pipeline: three-class OOF drops from 89.5\% to
84.0\% ($-5.5$pp) and binary from 95.9\% to 92.9\% ($-3.0$pp). The
deployed fusion weights were not re-tuned on the clean channels, so
these corrected numbers are conservative lower bounds on the honest
OOF rather than re-optimised estimates.

\paragraph{Duplicate-text leakage (fold-isolation violation).}
Audit of \texttt{reviews\_600\_base.jsonl} shows gold-600 contains 600
rows but only 592 unique texts: 8 exact-duplicate pairs (ids
\{63,101\}, \{64,109\}, \{68,110\}, \{71,115\}, \{75,117\}, \{82,118\},
\{99,104\}, \{100,108\}). The duplicates were \emph{not} removed
before training or evaluation, and every OOF script uses
\texttt{StratifiedKFold} without \texttt{groups=}, so duplicate texts
can land in different folds---with 5 folds we expect
$\approx$6--7 of the 8 pairs to be split. This violates level-(i)
per-row fold isolation at the text level even though the CV
machinery itself is correct. We estimate the resulting accuracy
inflation at roughly 1--2pp, concentrated in the neutral and negative
classes. The deduplicated rerun on the 592 unique texts
(\texttt{scripts/dedup\_retrain.sh},
\texttt{scripts/eval\_dedup\_pipeline.py}) moves the $\alpha{=}1.0$
estimates to 82.6--83.1\% three-class / 74.8--75.9 macro-F1 and
91.5\% / 90.8 binary (from 83.3--84.0 / 76.3--77.5 and 92.9 / 92.4 on
the 600 rows), consistent with the $\sim$1pp estimate
(\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Selection bias (ood1000 as development set).}
Git history shows the active-learning study iterated headline
metrics on ood1000 across roughly two hours of commits, with the
reported configuration changing four times. The final
\texttt{mix\_crossfit.py} performs 5-fold CV \emph{on ood1000 itself}:
each fold's weights are honest, but every one of the 1{,}000 labels
influenced the selection. Beyond weights, the AL scale ladder
(1K$\to$17K), teacher provenance (DeepSeek/Qwen/hybrid), epoch
count (e4 vs.\ e8), seed subsets (avg4 vs.\ avg5), and specialist
mixes were all compared on ood1000. The reported 77.1\% three-class /
92.3\% binary numbers are therefore best-of-$N$ maxima over
$\approx$26 mix configurations and $\approx$73 SFT runs; we estimate
the selection optimism at $+0.3$ to $+1.0$pp by comparing the chosen
configuration against runner-up configurations in the released log.

\paragraph{Text-level overlap audit.}
Identifier-disjointness does not rule out text-level exposure through
re-crawled, reconstructed, or derivative training pools. We audited
all 400 human400 texts against every project training pool with three
checks: (i) exact text match; (ii) normalized text match (lowercased,
punctuation stripped); and (iii) near-duplicate detection via
character 3-gram Jaccard similarity $>0.8$ over 462{,}879 unique pool
texts. Targets included gold-600, all lab2000 pools (ood1000, al1000),
all AL expansion pools, the pseudo-label and feature manifests, and
the reconstructed corpus training sets.

Against the training pools of the systems evaluated on human400 (the
v3 ensemble channels, the al17k mixture, Qwen3-4B, and the
star-rating lookup), exact overlap is 0/400 and near-duplicate overlap
($J{>}0.8$) is 0/400. Normalized matching flags two rows---both under
12 normalized characters of boilerplate text---that also occur in
corpus pseudo-label pools feeding the deployed neutral-detector
channel, and three rows occur in the full 910{,}998-review corpus
under different comment identifiers. Text-level exposure is therefore
bounded to two ultra-short boilerplate rows in one ensemble channel.
Pools with substantial exact overlap (46--145 rows) exist only in
rating-derived TF-IDF and corpus-scale MacBERT experiments that were
not among the systems evaluated on human400, so they do not affect
the reported comparison.

\section{Annotation Protocol and Guidelines}
\label{app:annotation}

\subsection{Gold-600}
Reviews were scraped from the Douban page of a single film (first
30 comment pages; 600 rows covering 592 unique texts, including 8
exact-duplicate pairs that were \emph{not} removed before training or
evaluation---see the duplicate-text leakage analysis in
Appendix~\ref{app:audit}). Two label
systems were recorded: a \emph{star-rule} label derived from the
reviewer's star rating and a \emph{semantic} label assigned by the
authors under written guidelines. The semantic label is authoritative
for all reported metrics. Disagreement between the two systems is
analysed in \S\ref{sec:data}.

\subsection{lab2000 / AL adjudication}
The ood1000 and al1000 sets were labelled in two passes by the same
LLM annotator under a fixed prompt, then disagreements were
adjudicated by the authors. Provenance is recorded per row
(\texttt{final\_source}: \texttt{consensus} vs.\ \texttt{user}). A
known limitation: during adjudication the student model's predictions
were visible in the interface, which may anchor human labels toward
model outputs; we flag this wherever adjudicated rows are reported
separately.

\subsection{New 400-item human test set}
To obtain an identifier-disjoint evaluation we sampled 400 reviews from
the 911K corpus with length stratification (160 short $\le$20 chars,
120 medium 21--50, 80 long 51--100, 40 extra-long $>$100) and
\emph{excluded every comment ID} appearing in gold-600, lab2000,
lab500, fresh500, the AL pools, or the confident pseudo-label pools
($\approx$270K rows). A post-hoc audit re-verified zero identifier
overlap against every corpus-side training-pool manifest; a
text-level overlap audit (exact, normalized, near-duplicate) against
all project training pools is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:human400}. Because the sample is length-stratified,
it is benchmark-specific rather than a population sample of the
natural corpus distribution. Items were
labelled blind (no model predictions shown) under the written semantic
guidelines by a single author; optional flags marked mixed, off-topic,
spoiler, and uncertain rows. The written guidelines and the self-contained
offline annotation page used for labelling are released under
\texttt{annotation/}. Because only one annotator was available,
no inter-annotator agreement is computable on this set.

\section{Complete Negative-Results List}
\label{app:negative}

Over twenty architecture and data variants were evaluated and
rejected during the project; the deployed configuration is the
measured optimum. Selected rejections most relevant to the audit:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item LoRA fine-tuning of the MegatronBERT backbone: binary
        0.9390/0.9343, three-class 0.8083/0.6431---hurts the ensemble.
  \item Median / trimmed / geometric / logit-mean aggregation:
        all worse than plain probability mean.
  \item Per-channel weight re-tuning: equal weights optimal within
        $\pm$0.1pp noise.
  \item 3-class student grid (alpha, tau, seed, epochs, class
        weights, Qwen-augmented teacher): all worse than the deployed
        $\alpha{=}0.5$, $\tau{=}2$, e8 configuration.
  \item Synthetic-review augmentation (Qwen3-4B generated, 75
        self-consistent rows): poisoned training; student OOF dropped
        to 0.7917.
  \item Round-2 pseudo-label flywheel: student probabilities degenerate
        (mean confidence 0.986)---the flywheel saturates after one
        round.
  \item Corpus-trained MLP heads on 1.3B features (75K pseudo rows):
        all ensemble variants worse than gold-trained channels.
  \item Cascade threshold on gold-600: split-half validation collapses
        ($\sim$0.86--0.88 vs.\ 0.89 tuned)---small-sample threshold
        overfitting.
  \item Stacked logistic-regression meta-learner on channel
        log-probabilities: 0.8367/0.7936, far below the deployed
        blend; channels too correlated for 600 samples.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ethics Statement and Data Availability}
\label{app:ethics}

\paragraph{Data.}
Review texts are Douban user comments. The gold set derives from a
single public film page; the corpus derives from the Moviedata-10M
release (research-use dataset, attribution required, raw data not
redistributed by us). We did not obtain platform authorization; the
release is limited to $\approx$5.0K evaluation/benchmark reviews with
user identifiers removed, for research purposes. Approximately 18.5K
unique review texts were sent to the DeepSeek API for teacher
labelling; all 600 gold texts were processed on Alibaba Cloud PAI.
No review text is redistributed beyond the released benchmark files.
Users were not contacted; we judge privacy risk low because only
public short reviews are involved, but acknowledge this is a
limitation of the study design.

\paragraph{Artifacts.}
Code, audit scripts, per-row provenance fields, OOF probability
files, and the configuration log are publicly released (repository
and model URLs withheld for double-blind review; available upon
request). License: code MIT; data for research use only.

\paragraph{Intended use.}
The system is a research prototype for sentiment analysis of Chinese
reviews; it is not intended for moderation decisions affecting users.
